A Story of Courage, Compassion & Faith

★

Some acts of courage happen far from the battlefield. Others take place in the darkest moments of human suffering.

Saving Otto Brune by Ross Lindberg tells the remarkable story of his father, Sergeant Edward G. Lindberg, a US Army medic who served during World War II.

Guided by his Christian faith, Edward dedicated himself to saving lives, even those of enemy soldiers. His most unforgettable experience came on July 16, 1945, when a devastating train collision near Aßling, Germany, brought him face-to-face with a dying German prisoner of war.

What happened that night became a lifelong lesson in compassion, forgiveness, and the power of faith.

A Life Defined by Courage, Compassion & Faith

★

Sergeant Edward G. Lindberg was more than a World War II veteran. He was a devoted husband, loving father, faithful Christian, and American Army medic who believed every human life deserved compassion.

Born in Greeley, Colorado, in 1912, Edward grew up in a Swedish-American family. At nineteen, he embraced Christianity and committed his life to serving God.

When America entered World War II, Edward chose to serve his country without carrying a weapon. As a conscientious objector and trained medic, he treated wounded soldiers throughout the European campaign.

His courage earned him the Bronze Star Medal, while his compassion left a legacy that reached far beyond the war.

Through his father's memories, personal photographs, and historical research, Ross Lindberg preserves the story of a man who demonstrated that true strength can be found in kindness, forgiveness, and faith.

Saving Otto Brune

Memoirs of a US Army Medic in World War II One American medic. One devastating night. One extraordinary lesson in compassion.

Based on the wartime memories of Sergeant Edward G. Lindberg, Saving Otto Brune explores the human side of World War II through an unforgettable story of courage, sacrifice, and faith.

At its heart is Edward's encounter with a dying German prisoner after the Aßling train disaster of July 16, 1945. Ross Lindberg believes the soldier was Otto Brune, an identification he presents as personal testimony rather than established historical fact.

Combining family memories, historical photographs, and documented wartime events, this moving memoir honors an American medic who chose mercy when confronted with hatred.
