The Book & The Author

Saving Otto Brune

★

Saving Otto Brune brings together the wartime memories surrounding Sgt. Edward G. Lindberg, a U.S. Army Medical Technician (MOS 409) who served in the European campaign during World War II. More than a battlefield medic, Lindberg was a devoted Christian whose courage, compassion and faith guided him as he treated the wounded, comforted the suffering, and shared the hope of Christ amid war.

For readers of World War II history, military medicine and inspirational biography, the book honors a medic who sought not only to save lives, but to win souls.

World War II History · Memoir · Inspirational

Author’s Note

★
This is a true story, told as my father told it to me, and as I have been able to confirm it. My father’s Army records were lost in the 1973 fire at the National Personnel Records Center in St. Louis, so some details come from memory, family and faith, and not from paperwork. Where the documents do exist, such as his Bronze Star citation and the German historical research on the train wreck in Aßling, I have used them.

Some names in this book have been changed, and I have marked those where it matters. — Ross Lindberg

Dedication

★

To the men and women who gallantly served our nation in World War II, and especially to the soldiers in the backline units: the Red Ball Express, the nurses and medical staff, the Military Police, and the administrative staff. My father told me that ten men stood behind every man on the front line.

And to every young person who reads this book: may you gain a greater appreciation for my parents’ generation, and for how they overcame great trials to win a greater victory for the future of our world. May this story lead you to Jesus Christ, and to a righteous life that follows His example.
