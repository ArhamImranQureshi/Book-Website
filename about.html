Saving Otto Brune

★

Memoirs of a US Army Medic in World War II

Saving Otto Brune by Ross Lindberg tells the remarkable story of Sergeant Edward G. Lindberg, a US Army medic whose faith guided him through the hardships of World War II.

Born in Greeley, Colorado, Edward served his country as a conscientious objector. Rather than carry a weapon, he chose to save lives. His medical service took him across Europe, where he treated wounded soldiers and earned the Bronze Star Medal for his courageous work.

The book's most powerful moment unfolds on July 16, 1945, during a devastating train collision near Aßling, Germany. Amid the wreckage, Edward stayed beside a dying German soldier who initially rejected his help. Instead of turning away, he offered medical care, comfort, and prayer.

Ross believes the soldier was Otto Brune, although his identity remains unconfirmed by historical records.

Through personal memories, wartime photographs, and historical research, this moving World War II memoir explores the lasting power of courage, compassion, forgiveness, and Christian faith.

For readers of World War II history, military medicine and inspirational biography, the book honors a medic who sought not only to save lives, but to win souls.

About Ross Lindberg

★

Ross Lindberg was born in 1959 in Greeley, Colorado. He is the youngest son of Edward and Edith Lindberg.

Growing up, Ross developed a close relationship with his father through fishing, golf, and conversations about World War II. These memories inspired him to preserve Edward's remarkable experiences for future generations.

Ross has worked in information technology since 1981. He lives in Longmont, Colorado, with his wife, Rosa, and remains devoted to his Christian faith.

With Saving Otto Brune, Ross honors his father's legacy and shares an inspiring message about treating others with compassion, even in the darkest moments.

Author's Note

From Ross Lindberg
★

"This is a true story, told as my father told it to me, and as I have been able to confirm it. My father's Army records were lost in the 1973 fire at the National Personnel Records Center in St. Louis, so some details come from memory, family and faith, and not from paperwork. Where the documents do exist, such as his Bronze Star citation and the German historical research on the train wreck in Aßling, I have used them.

One part of this story rests on faith more than on a document: my belief that the dying soldier my father held in July 1945 was a man named Otto Brune. I explain in Chapter 8 how I came to that conviction. I ask you to read it as my testimony.

Some names in this book have been changed, and I have marked those where it matters."

Ross Lindberg

Dedication

Honoring Those Who Served
★

"To the men and women who gallantly served our nation in World War II, and especially to the soldiers in the backline units: the Red Ball Express, the nurses and medical staff, the Military Police, and the administrative staff. My father told me that ten men stood behind every man on the front line.

And to every young person who reads this book: may you gain a greater appreciation for my parents' generation, and for how they overcame great trials to win a greater victory for the future of our world. May this story lead you to Jesus Christ, and to a righteous life that follows His example."
